Essentially a lost world story, part of which takes place underground in Morocco. Two other tales are included in this translation, of which his 'Memoirs of a Gorilla' is surprisingly good.

Malato himself was an anarchist, who in 1875 had been deported to New Caledonia with his family, his father being a communard. From 1878 he was a friend of Louise Michel, who in 1885 published the other short piece included here, 'The Rat and the Octopus'. Amnestied in 1881, Malato returned to France, where he made his living as a reporter, pamphleteer, and writer. In 1888 he published his _Philosophy of Anarchy_. Two years later he was sentenced to 15 months imprisonment for an article allegedly "inciting murder, pillage and arson." In 1892, following anarchist bombings, he took refuge in London. The following year, with Errico Malatesta, he went to Belgium to fight for universal suffrage. Back in France, in 1905, with five other anarchists, he was accused of an attempt on the life of Alfonso XIII, the king of Spain, but lack of evidence led to acquittal, following six months' remand. Michel Shreve, his translator, writes in his introduction that "Along with the man struggling to stay free we find united in him the missionary of anarchy and unrepentant agitator."
